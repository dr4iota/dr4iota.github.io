These pages are for Telechore 0.6.0 — what's new in it.

Games

Telechore includes Termoasis's console games, such as 2048, Sudoku, Mines, Snake and Life. They are drawn with the characters of a terminal. They run in the app itself, with no server and no connection, on every platform.

“Games” is the last section of the start page, and of the sidebar. It has one row, “Termoasis console games”, which opens the welcome menu.

The welcome menu and the picker

The welcome menu lists every game by kind: puzzles, arcade games and simulations. Move to a game and press “Play” (or Enter) to start it.

The name at the top of the page is also the picker. Tap it to switch to any game, including in the middle of one, or to go back to “Welcome”. “Up”, to the left of the name (or the first item in “⋯”), quits the game and returns to the welcome menu, still on the game you left.

Playing

On a touch screen each game draws its own keys under the board, or beside it when the screen is held sideways. The arrows are on the right, the game's own actions (Undo, Restart, Place, Drop…) are on the left, and games that take numbers have a number pad. With a keyboard, those on-screen keys are hidden and the game's keyboard shortcuts work. “Keys” in the header shows or hides the on-screen keys either way.

Some games are steered by a finger on the board. In Paddles, drag to move the paddle.

Levels and modes

A game's levels, difficulty or mode — Warehouse's levels, Mines' difficulty, Chaos's mode — is a stepper. “‹” and “›” step through the choices, and a tap on the current value lists them all. The stepper sits in the header when there is room. Otherwise it sits in a band under the header, or beside the keys when the screen is turned sideways. “⋯” lists every setting the game has, and each one opens its list. The game's own keys still work. On a command line, set level 12 does the same.

“Help” shows the game's own help: its keys, and the commands it takes. “Command” opens a line for one command, such as family julia in Fractals or pattern glider in Life. Each game's help says which commands it has.

If a game needs more room than the screen has, it is drawn smaller, down to a font that can still be read. If it still does not fit, it says how much room it needs. Mines on Expert, on a phone held upright, is the case you are most likely to see.

When the header has room, “Keep”, “Keys”, “Help” and “Command” sit in it. On a narrow screen they are behind “⋯”, or beside the level control under the header. With the screen turned sideways, they sit at the top of the keys when there is room.

Keeping a game

“Keep” (the anchor) in the header keeps a game running while you go elsewhere, in the same way “Keep” keeps a terminal. Leave the page, press “Up”, or pick another game, and the kept game waits where you left it. You come back to it from the strip of “Kept” games on the welcome menu, or from a terminal's list. A game you did not keep closes when you leave it. A game that has ended is replaced by the next one.

Kept games do not count towards the number of terminals the free edition may keep running, and quitting the app does not ask about them.

A kept game lasts only as long as the app does. Closing the app, or Android closing it in the background, ends the game, just as that ends a kept terminal. What a game has saved stays: every game's best score.

In a browser

The same games can be played on Telechore's website, under “Play”, in any current browser. Saves there stay in the browser.
