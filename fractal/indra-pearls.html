Indra's pearls

Take four disks in two pairs. Each pair makes one map, a rule that moves every point of the plane: it takes everything outside one disk of the pair and squeezes it into the other disk, turning it as it goes. Run backward, the same map takes everything outside the second disk into the first. The maps are Möbius maps, which send circles to circles.

Because the outside of a disk holds the other three disks, squeezing it into its partner puts a small copy of each of those three inside the partner. Then each small copy holds three smaller copies, and so on, without end: 4 disks, then 12 copies inside them, then 36, each generation three times the last. (With more maps there are more disks, and each holds a copy of every disk but the one it is paired with.) The pattern repeats inside itself the way every pearl in Indra's net reflects every other pearl. The net is an image from Buddhist philosophy, and Mumford, Series and Wright named their 2002 book about these pictures, Indra's Pearls, after it.

Every point of the plane is folded back out of the disks. If it lies in a disk, it is moved by the map that undoes the squeeze which filled that disk, and this repeats until the point lies outside all the disks. Points that get out are colored by how many steps they took and by where they came out, which gives the colors their swirl. The points that never get out form the limit set, the pearls, drawn black.

The disks never overlap, and in this case the limit set is a dust: it is so finely scattered that no two of its points are joined by a line inside it. The maps and everything built from them make a Schottky group, after Friedrich Schottky, who studied such groups in the 1870s. Robert Fricke and Felix Klein drew pictures like these by hand in their books on the subject around 1900.

Parameters

GUI Parameters

  • Iterations: How many steps a point may take before it counts as one of the pearls.
  • Generators: How many maps there are, from 2 to 4. Each map has a pair of disks.
  • Disk radius: The size of the disks, all the same. It is made smaller when the disks would overlap, so they can come almost to touch.
  • Twist: The angle, in degrees, by which each map turns what it moves. The copies inside each disk turn with it.

Control Points

  • Source 1 and Target 1, Source 2 and Target 2, …: The centers of the disks, in pairs. Each map takes the outside of its source disk into its target disk. At the start the first pair lies on the horizontal and the second on the vertical.
