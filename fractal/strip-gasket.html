Gasket in a strip

Draw two parallel lines one unit apart, and between them a row of circles of diameter 1, one at every whole number, each touching both lines and its two neighbors. Under each pair of neighbors there is a curved gap against the lower line, and over them one against the upper line. Fill every gap with the circle that touches its three sides, then the new, smaller gaps, and so on without end.

Every packing between two parallel lines is this one, moved, turned or scaled, so there is nothing to drag. Look at it as the gasket with two of its four first circles straightened into lines: in the gasket on a line, make circles A and B equal, and this is what appears.

Ford circles

The lower half holds the Ford circles. For every fraction p/q in lowest terms, one circle touches the lower line at x=p/q, and its radius is

r=2q21​.

The circles at the whole numbers have q=1. Between the circles at a/b and c/d, the circle that fills the gap sits at their mediant (a+c)/(b+d): between 0/1 and 1/1 it is at 1/2, then 1/3 and 2/3, and so on. Two Ford circles touch exactly when their fractions are neighbors in this way, with ∣ad−bc∣=1. The upper half is the same picture upside down.

Colors

The colors follow the same rule as the Apollonian gasket. The lines have the value 0 and the row of circles has 1. Each new circle gets the average of the three it touches, weighted by curvature to the power p, times the ratio f. The lines have curvature 0, so at p>0 they do not count at all.
