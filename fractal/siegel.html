Persistent Siegel disks

A Siegel disk is a region around a fixed point on which the map acts as an irrational rotation: every orbit inside turns forever on its own closed curve. For a quadratic polynomial z2+c it is a bounded disk, as in the Julia set for the golden-mean c≈−0.3905−0.5868i. R. Berenguel and N. Fagella studied a family of entire transcendental maps that keep such a disk for every value of their parameter a=0:

fa​(z)=λa(ez/a(z+1−a)+a−1),λ=e2πiθ, θ=25​−1​.

The fixed point 0 has multiplier fa′​(0)=λ, a rotation by the golden mean, so 0 is always the center of a Siegel disk. The map has one free critical point, z=−1 (since fa′​(z)=λez/a(z+1)), and one asymptotic value, λa(a−1), which fa​ approaches wherever ez/a→0.

The parameter a moves the family between two familiar worlds. As a→∞ the map tends to the quadratic polynomial λ(z+z2/2), and the picture looks like a quadratic Siegel Julia set (try a=15−15i). As a→0 it behaves like the exponential map λ(eu−1) in u=z/a, and at a=1 it is λzez. In between the Siegel disk can become unbounded, or its boundary an indecomposable continuum: the default a=−0.33258+0.10324i is such a case.

An entire map has no escape radius: a point far out where ez/a→0 falls back near the asymptotic value. A point is counted as escaping once Re(z/a)>20, where the exponential takes over, as is usual for exponential maps. The escaping points form thin "hairs"; everything else, including the Siegel disk and all its preimages, is the bounded part.

Parameters

GUI Parameters

  • Iterations: This integer value determines the maximum number of times the formula is applied for each point. Higher values increase the detail and accuracy of the fractal.
  • Interior: Shades the points whose orbits never escape, otherwise black, in dark grays by the average step ∣zk+1​−zk​∣ of their orbits; higher values give more bands, and 0 turns it off. It starts on here: inside the Siegel disk the orbits turn on invariant curves, which show as rings.

Complex Parameters (Control Points)

  • a: The family's parameter. Drag it to move between the quadratic-like maps (large ∣a∣) and the exponential-like ones (small ∣a∣); λ stays fixed, so the Siegel disk at 0 persists for every a.

Reference: R. Berenguel, N. Fagella, An entire transcendental family with a persistent Siegel disc, J. Difference Equ. Appl. (2010); arXiv:0907.0116.
