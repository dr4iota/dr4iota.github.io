Lattice pearls

Stand four mirrors around a square, so that the square's reflections tile the whole plane, and put one round mirror, a disc, inside it. A flat mirror reflects a point across a line; a round mirror inverts it: a point inside the disc goes to the point outside it on the same ray from the center, at distance r2/d when it was at distance d, where r is the radius.

Every point of the plane is first reflected back into the square, the cell. If it lands inside the disc, it is inverted out of it, reflected back into the cell again, and so on. Most points get out after a few steps, and they are colored by how many steps they took and by where they left the disc. The points that never get out form the limit set, drawn black.

The disc's reflections in the mirrors fill the plane with copies of it, one in every cell. Inverting in the disc shrinks all those copies into small discs inside it, and they in turn hold smaller copies, and so on: rings of pearls inside pearls. While the disc keeps clear of the mirrors, the limit set is a fine dust. Bring the disc up to a mirror and the pearls crowd together near the point where they touch, where points need more and more steps to get out.

With Mirrors at 1, only the two upright mirrors are kept, and the picture repeats along a band instead of in both directions.

Parameters

GUI Parameters

  • Iterations: How many inversions a point may take before it counts as staying.
  • Mirrors: 2 for the four mirrors around the square (a lattice), 1 for the two upright ones only (a frieze).

Control Points

  • Center: The center of the disc. It stays inside the cell −1≤x,y≤1.
  • Rim: A point on the edge of the disc; its distance from the center is the radius, which stays between 0.02 and 1.
