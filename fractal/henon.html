Hénon basin

The Hénon map moves each point (x,y) of the plane to a new point:

(x,y)↦(1−ax2+y, bx)

Here the plane is not the complex numbers: x and y are two ordinary real numbers, and a and b are two fixed real numbers. Michel Hénon chose this map in 1976 as the simplest one whose orbits settle onto a strange attractor, a thin curve folded over and over on itself.

Every pixel is a starting point, and the map is applied to it again and again. Most starting points run off to infinity, and they are colored by how quickly they go. The others stay near the attractor forever: together they make its basin, drawn black.

For many values of a and b the edge of the basin is a smooth curve. For others, such as the starting values a=1.39, b=−0.3, it is a fractal: zoom in on the edge and fingers of escaping points keep reaching into the basin at every scale. Moving (a,b) changes the edge from smooth to fractal and back. Hénon's own values, a=1.4 and b=0.3, give a smooth edge.

Unlike the other pictures in this group, the map can be run backwards: from the new point you can always find the old one, because b=0 lets you solve for x.

Parameters

GUI Parameters

  • Iterations: How many times the map is applied to each point before the point counts as staying in the basin.
  • Interior: Shades the points of the basin, otherwise black, in dark grays by the average step of their orbits; 0 (the default) turns it off.

Control Point

  • (a, b): The two numbers of the map, read as the point's two coordinates. Drag it to change both at once.
