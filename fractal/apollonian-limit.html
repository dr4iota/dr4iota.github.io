Apollonian limit set

Four circles touch one another: three inside, and the outer one around them. Each is a round mirror. Reflecting in a round mirror, called inversion, swaps its inside and its outside: a point at distance d from the center goes to the point on the same ray at distance r2/d, where r is the radius.

The four circles leave four gaps between them, each a triangle with curved sides. Every point of the picture is folded back into a gap. If it lies inside one of the three inner circles, it is reflected in that circle, which takes it outside; it may then land in another circle, or outside the outer circle, and be reflected again. The point stops as soon as it reaches a gap, and it is colored by the number of reflections that took. The gaps themselves need none. Outside the outer circle is not part of the picture and stays black.

Reflecting a gap in a circle puts a smaller copy of it inside that circle, and reflecting the copies puts smaller copies inside those. So each circle fills with smaller and smaller curved triangles, and the color changes with every level.

Some points never reach a gap. These form the limit set, drawn black, and it is not a scattering of dust but a connected lace through the points where the circles touch. Near a touching point a reflected point is passed from one circle to its neighbor and back, taking more and more steps to get out.

The lace is itself an Apollonian gasket, though not of these four circles. Through the three corners of each gap there is one more circle, which crosses the three circles around that gap at right angles. These four new circles also all touch, and the gasket they start is the black lace. At the start, three touching points lie on one line, so one of the new circles is a straight line, drawn black across the middle. The gasket of the original four circles is the Apollonian gasket item.

Inside each of the new circles, the gap and its copies are curved triangles whose sides cross that circle at right angles and whose corners lie on it. They tile the circle the way Kaleido's curved tilings fill the Poincaré disk: a tiling of the hyperbolic plane, here by triangles with their corners on the rim.

Parameters

GUI Parameters

  • Iterations: How many reflections a point may take before it counts as part of the limit set.

Control Points

  • Center A, Center B, Center C: The centers of the three inner circles. Their radii are set so that each pair touches, and the outer circle is the one that holds all three and touches each. A drag stops where the three would no longer fit inside one circle.
